# Creative Brief Template

## Creative Brief Template

Use the fields below as the output structure for every creative brief. Fill each field with specific content; do not leave placeholders.

### 1. Background
- One or two sentences describing the context: what is happening now, why the idea is relevant now, and what prompted the request.
- Example: "The coffee shop has seen a 20% drop in afternoon sales and wants to re-engage office workers with a plant-based option."

### 2. Objective
- State the primary, measurable goal.
- Choose one primary objective from: awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, or advocacy.
- Example: "Drive 500 new orders of the oat milk latte in the first 30 days."

### 3. Target Audience
- Define the primary audience with 3–5 concrete attributes: demographic, behavior, need, barrier, and motivation.
- Use this pattern: "Who they are, what they currently do, what they want, what blocks them, what would change their behavior."
- Example: "Young professionals, age 25–35, who already buy coffee daily but default to dairy. They want a lighter option that still feels indulgent. Barrier: doubt about plant-milk taste. Motivation: a familiar café ritual without the heaviness."

### 4. Key Insight
- A single sentence that captures the underlying human truth the creative will use.
- Example: "People don't choose oat milk for health; they choose it to feel like they are making a smart modern choice without sacrificing comfort."

### 5. Big Idea
- The central theme that unites every execution.
- Example: "Your afternoon, lightened."

### 6. Message Pillars
- 2–3 proof points that support the big idea.
- Example: (1) Creamy texture indistinguishable from dairy; (2) only 3 ingredients; (3) crafted for afternoon energy without the crash.

### 7. Tone and Voice
- Select one primary tone from: warm, playful, bold, sophisticated, authoritative, empathetic, or energetic.
- Add one or two supporting modifiers.
- Example: "Warm and optimistic, with a hint of wit."

### 8. Mandatories
- Non-negotiable elements that must appear: logo, tagline, URL, disclaimer, legal line, hashtag, or brand colors.
- Example: "#CleanAfternoon tag, shop logo, and plant-based certification mark."

### 9. Call to Action
- The single action the audience should take.
- Example: "Order the oat milk latte via the app and get 20% off your first afternoon order."

### 10. Deliverables and Formats
- List each asset, its format, and the channel where it will live.
- Example: 15-second vertical video for Instagram Reels and TikTok, 3 static carousel cards, 1 in-store poster, 1 email announcement.

### 11. Success Metrics
- 2–4 metrics tied directly to the objective.
- Example: orders in the first 30 days, coupon redemption rate, social engagement rate, and repeat afternoon purchase rate.

### 12. Timeline and Dependencies
- Key milestones in order, plus what must be true before production can start.
- Example: final script by day 3, shoot by day 7, content approval by day 10, launch by day 14. Dependencies: product photography, app coupon setup, legal review of claims.

## Notes for the Agent

- Fill every field with specific content derived from the user's input.
- If a field cannot be filled from known information, add an explicit `Assumption:` label before the content.
- Do not use vague statements such as "target audience: everyone." Narrow the audience until the message can be written specifically for them.
