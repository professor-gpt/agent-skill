# Reference: Creative Frameworks

## Frameworks

### Before/After/Bridge (BAB)
- Structure: show the "before" state, reveal the "after" state, then present the bridge that gets from one to the other.
- Use when: the campaign must demonstrate transformation or relief.
- Example: "Before: afternoon slump. After: calm, steady focus. Bridge: the new oat milk latte."

### Story Spine
- Structure:
  1. Once upon a time...
  2. Every day...
  3. But one day...
  4. Because of that...
  5. Because of that...
  6. Until finally...
  7. And ever since then...
- Use when: the creative needs a narrative arc, a brand story, or a character-driven spot.
- Example: "Once upon a time there was a busy professional. Every day she bought a heavy afternoon drink. But one day she tried the new oat milk latte. Because of that she felt light and focused. Because of that she started a new afternoon ritual. Until finally she stopped hitting the 3 p.m. slump. And ever since then, afternoons taste better."

### SCAMPER
- Seven operations for improving an existing product, service, or campaign idea:
  - **S**ubstitute — replace one element with another.
  - **C**ombine — merge two features or concepts.
  - **A**dapt — adjust an existing idea for a new context.
  - **M**odify — change the size, shape, or intensity.
  - **P**ut to another use — use it in an unexpected way.
  - **E**liminate — remove a part to simplify.
  - **R**everse/Rearrange — flip the sequence or emphasis.
- Use when: the task is to improve an existing concept or generate variations.

### Six Thinking Hats
- White hat — facts and data.
- Red hat — emotions and gut feeling.
- Black hat — risks and weaknesses.
- Yellow hat — benefits and optimism.
- Green hat — new ideas and alternatives.
- Blue hat — process and control.
- Use when: evaluating a creative idea or running a structured review.

### Big Idea Test
- A strong big idea passes five checks:
  1. It can be expressed in one sentence.
  2. It can be executed in at least 10 different formats.
  3. It contains a tension or unexpected connection.
  4. It is specific to the brand or product, not generic.
  5. It makes the audience feel something, not just understand something.
- Use when: selecting the best concept among candidate directions.

## Creative Direction Pattern

Each creative direction must include:

- **Name** — a memorable label for the direction.
- **Emotional register** — the primary feeling the audience should experience.
- **Core message** — one sentence the direction communicates.
- **Execution example** — a concrete sample asset or scene.
- **Tone** — the voice and attitude used in copy and visuals.
- **Audience fit** — why this direction would resonate with the defined audience.

## Quality Checklist

Before returning the creative plan, verify:

1. Every Creative Brief field in `./templates/creative-brief.md` is filled.
2. The three directions differ meaningfully in at least two of: core message, emotional register, tone, or format.
3. The big idea passes the Big Idea Test in this file.
4. No direction relies on unlicensed music, stock imagery, or protected slogans.
5. All claims are either sourced or labeled as assumptions.
6. The production roadmap lists dependencies in the correct sequence.
